The current author has 320 characters for the entire problem. The question-quality audit identified missing conditions but has not established that this limit caused them. This experiment compares the existing limit with 1200 characters while keeping the author model, goal, reference packet, other instructions, and solver/reviewer unchanged. It tests author guidance and matching admission length together. It does not isolate prompting from admission policy.

Two ordinary goals exercise applied reasoning: HTML disabled state and constraint validation, and standard chess castling. Each arm requests three questions per goal at minimum difficulty 3. Both arms receive the same manually prepared primary-source summaries. The author receives no expected question, answer key, adjudication checklist, or external control example. These two selected goals are a diagnostic, not evidence of arbitrary-subject coverage or automatic retrieval.

The distinction between case information and the question itself follows established item-writing practice. NBME recommends focused questions supported by relevant case information and comparable, plausible alternatives. That guidance informs this experiment; its health-science examples do not establish a universal character budget or guarantee our generation quality. ## Frozen operation

The dedicated [evaluation runner](../backend/bedrock-question-service/evals/checkpoint_stem_length_eval.py) prepares an exact plan, then requires its canonical hash to execute. The plan binds source revision and hashes, dependencies, fixture, prompts, normalized requests, model settings, and allowances. The expanded author prompt changes only the stem-limit numeral; the separate 320-character author explanation limit stays unchanged.

The [frozen dispatch plan](evidence/stem-length-plan-20260907.json) was prepared and revalidated without inference on September 7 at source revision `22980c1dbfb8875512f8acdd27664c9993d4d064`. Its canonical SHA-256 is `3462043fb316b763017feb0421a0bac6354ef2b4413b5a3ca1abc3ed48ea0f72`. Execution must use that exact source revision and recorded dependencies in an isolated process; later documentation commits do not change the frozen source. This artifact records prospective inputs, not model results.

There are four counterbalanced jobs and at most twelve provider calls: one author, one stem-only solver, and one reviewer per job. A gate may skip later calls. The configuration is the already available `us.anthropic.claude-opus-4-6-v1`, adaptive thinking at high effort, and 16,000 maximum output tokens per call. SDK attempts are one; connect/read timeouts are 3/100 seconds. No repair, retry, replacement case, or inventory top-up is permitted. Provider or required-format failure stops execution; content failures prevent promotion while the remaining predeclared jobs continue.

Requests are saved before dispatch. Final response text, usage when known, stop reasons, and elapsed times are captured. The runner retains every raw candidate, the exact admitted and returned text, and separate observations of what the unchanged production 320-character sanitizer would do. Its temporary length override exists only in the evaluation process. This run does not serve questions or change the phone, deployed worker, or existing bank.

## Assessment and interpretation

Review first hides keys, explanations, numeric difficulty, and pipeline verdicts. Length may reveal the arm, so this is not full arm blinding. Independent assessment covers literal completeness, all warranted choices, distractor distinctness and plausibility, learning-goal fit, meaningful scenario conditions, and cognitive difficulty. After freezing those findings, assess author and returned teaching feedback separately, including every choice explanation.

Report lengths and quality over all requested slots and actual raw candidates, separately from admitted and returned inventory. The expanded arm must actually use more than 320 characters for useful premises in at least one item per goal. Padding does not exercise the hypothesis. Its prospective feasibility threshold is three complete, useful questions with accurate feedback and independently assessed difficulty at least 3 in each goal. One-goal success is mixed evidence. Longer prose and more rules do not by themselves establish higher difficulty; each three-question batch is correlated.

Primary rules do not supply missing case facts. HTML cases need literal ancestry, attributes, operations, and the relevant API semantics. Chess cases need the relevant board and move-history facts; current piece locations do not establish castling rights. An incomplete authored representation must not be silently repaired during checking. The sources are the [WHATWG form-control definitions](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/form-control-infrastructure.html) and [FIDE Laws of Chess](https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023).

Before generation, separate assistant-created controls checked the local observation tools: [four browser cases](evidence/stem-length-html-preflight-20260906.json) matched the expected disabled/validation properties, and [six chess cases](evidence/stem-length-chess-preflight-20260906.json) replayed legal moves from the standard starting position and matched rights-versus-legality expectations. These controls are not model-authored questions or evidence of their quality. Any subsequent executable check must bind to the actual generated representation.

Even a successful content trial would leave presentation and integration work. The backend currently admits 320-character prompts, iOS admits 360, and mistake/history transport also uses bounded prompt fields. A new separate case field would need end-to-end preservation, identity, and client compatibility. The 100-second experimental read timeout also exceeds the worker's current 75-second setting. This experiment does not establish mobile usability, production latency, adaptive progression, calibrated mastery, or a release-ready correctness rate.
