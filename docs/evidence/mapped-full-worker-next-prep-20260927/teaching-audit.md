The [mechanical audit](mechanical-audit.json) independently computes the three quantitative keys and proves all 18 numeric within-item pairs have distinct values. The captured final questions use the exact learner text from the code-owned compiled/agreement sources. A separate post-lock check found exactly four nonempty feedback entries keyed by the four offered literals in every item, and no answer-position references under a bounded `choice|option|answer A–D` pattern across five explanations and 20 feedback entries. This supports shuffle safety but is not a general natural-language proof.

After unblinding, original slots 0–2 match the assigned exact-arithmetic objective and slots 3–4 match the assigned standard-English objective. The frozen request asked for three and two respectively at difficulty 2 or 3. Both blind reviewers selected the source key for all five items and judged every within-item choice pair meaningfully distinct. Reviewer A rated slot 3 difficulty 3; reviewer B rated it 2. Both ratings are inside the frozen allowed range. Both also flagged slots 3 and 4 as a strong near-duplicate in *format and tested skill*: each asks for two present-tense agreement forms and presents a complete 2×2 verb-pair grid. The subject structures differ (inversion versus gerund), and the literal answers differ, but the repeated template is a concrete batch-novelty limitation. The two reviewers also noticed moderate prompt-template repetition across numeric slots 1 and 2. These known limitations do not create a wrong key or duplicate answer choice; they should not be hidden by the narrow five-slot gate result.
