# Web grounding interface probe — September 6, 2026

One live call confirmed that the account can invoke Nova 2 Lite with its built-in web tool. It **did not qualify a source-evidence adapter or a correctness improvement**: three successful search observations were hidden, citations contained no source passages, and the generated explanation conflated requirements despite finding relevant primary references. No production code, model configuration, permissions or inventory changed.

## Frozen scope and result

## What independent source inspection found

The model's overall conclusion appropriately left room for missing visible context. Its explanation nevertheless suggested that programmatic accessible naming could satisfy SC 3.3.2 and treated association under SC 1.3.1 as a question to resolve for that criterion. The primary explanation explicitly separates those requirements: accessible names alone can coexist with failure when adequate labels or instructions are not presented to all users. Its later mention of that condition did not make the earlier teaching explanation clear or consistently correct. [W3C explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/labels-or-instructions.html)

The requested version was WCAG 2.2, but the returned citations were the [2.1 explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/labels-or-instructions.html) and an [unmaintained 2.0 page](https://www.w3.org/TR/UNDERSTANDING-WCAG20/minimize-error-cues). Independent inspection found the relevant distinction in 2.2 as well; that follow-up does not turn the original citation set into a version-matched retrieval. The model-written excerpts also joined source material and altered wording or punctuation. They were generated quotations, not source passages exposed by the tool. The response exceeded both its requested word limit and quotation limits without approaching the token cap.
